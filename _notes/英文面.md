# 自我介绍

Good morning, professors. My name is Yahao Ren. I study Software Engineering at Taiyuan University of Technology. I rank ninth among 1,538 students in my program. 

My research is about the safety of the data used to train large language models. Let me explain the problem in a simple way.

When we fine-tune a large model, we do not use every sample we collect. We use an automatic data selector to pick the samples that look most useful. Most people assume this selector also filters out bad data. My first project shows that the opposite can happen.

I call this attack "value hijacking". If an attacker writes poisoned samples in the style the selector prefers, the selector will rank them at the top and pick them first. So the filter that is supposed to protect the model ends up concentrating the poison instead. My method, AMRS, does this by only rewriting the surface form of the data — no gradients, no attack model. It made the poison more than five times denser than the strongest previous baseline. I built the whole project and wrote the paper. It is submitted to IEEE S&P, as first author.

My second project follows up on two questions: can we predict this risk before training, and can we defend against it without human labeling? I proposed a risk index for the first, and a defense for the second. The defense idea is simple — run two different selectors and keep only what they both agree on. It removed the poison completely in our experiments. This paper is submitted to AAAI, also as first author.

I also worked on two other topics. One is geometric prompt optimization for SAM .The other is conditional brain-image generation with UK Biobank data. 

Looking back, one question connects all of my work. When a system decides which data to trust, how can that decision be attacked, and how can we make it reliable? I would like to keep working in this direction in graduate school. Thank you.
